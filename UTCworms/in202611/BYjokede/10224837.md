# Worm by jokede: the rules AnCl3 takes from AnCl9

The conclusions AnCl3's rewritten docs rely on, published here as
`CLAUDE.md` 0.4 requires. The working behind them is in AnCl9, AnCl3's
private sister. They rest on the owners' rulings, made in conversation
with me and with `koa584`, AnCl9's drone, up to 2026-11-10.

## 1. The two repos

1. **AnCl3 holds the core both repos share.** AnCl9 cites it and adds its
   private differences, since a private repo can cite a public one but not
   the other way round. AnCl3 is increasingly AnCl9's public face.
2. **AnCl3 is brought up to date with AnCl9's constructions**, that is,
   with most of its rules.
3. **The deliberate differences:** (a) AnCl3 is public, AnCl9 private;
   (b) AnCl3 takes no transcripts yet; (c) AnCl3 is under Apache 2.0;
   (d) AnCl3's docs cite only AnCl3's worms; (e) drones' working, and for
   now their code, goes to AnCl9; (f) each repo has its own drones;
   (g) AnCl3 is notionally for Opal and Just17 (also sJust17), a simple
   chess-programming project for demonstration, and AnCl9 for a more
   serious one.
4. **The owners are named here only as "the owners".**
5. **The split will become legally significant in time**, but not yet.

## 2. The paradigm

1. **Nothing is hidden**, or as little as possible: everything about how the
   work is done lies open to view in the repo itself.
2. **Its facets:** write once, the universal principle, except for the
   docs, which are articulated by updating them; one branch, with history
   never rewritten; self-describing paths; secrets kept out at the door.
3. **Its distinctions:** truth and authority; new and old; use and
   mention; true and fair; duty and rank.

## 3. Tiers

1. **Root files and `moreMDs/`, then `draftMDs/`, then the wormstream.** A
   higher tier beats a lower one (lex superior), and weight goes with the
   tier, not the author. A worm is a rule only if a doc anoints it.
2. **Within a tier, the new supersedes the old** (lex posterior). Across
   authors, order comes from reference: the referring text is the newer.
3. **The owner in a conversation rules there** (lex specialis). Rulings are
   recorded in worms, and an override of a repo rule is confirmed with that
   owner first.
4. **Mistakes are overruled by new texts**, never by editing old ones.
5. **What may change where:** streams are add-only; root files and
   `moreMDs/` may be added to and updated but not deleted from; drafts may
   also be deleted, but are better retired in place. Where a no-update or
   no-delete rule bites, create instead.
6. **A commit message is its author's word**, and is corrected by a worm,
   never by rewriting it.

## 4. Roles

1. **The owners rule jointly.** A session doesn't blend apparent
   contradictions between them, but quotes both to the owner it's working
   with.
2. **Drones maintain; bees create.** It is a duty, not a rank.
3. **Drones may fill administrative gaps in other authors' folders**, such
   as a bundle's closing worm, or a marshalling drone's commits on the bees'
   behalf. That is editorship rather than authorship, and acceptable. The
   rules will adapt to such expedients as turn out to be needed.

## 5. Commits

1. **A subject line says what changed and cites its principal worm**,
   relative to the current month (`see ./AUTHOR/DDHHMMSS.md`).
2. **The body gives formalities first**, then the rationale, then trailers.
3. **Flags go in the subject line** of a commit to the author's own folder:
   `FAIL:`, `WARN:`, `NB:`, `BTW:`, `FYI:` and `FTR:`, most urgent first.
   Only `FAIL:` leads.

## 6. Bundles

1. **A bundle is meant to drop atomically.** When it can't, all adds come
   before any use; a mention is not a use.
2. **A bundle dropped over several commits is formally open** until a
   closing worm with its timestamp appears, beside it or inside it. A
   commit message saying it's done should prompt the operative drone to
   drop the closing worm in the right place. This replaces AnCl3's earlier
   rule that a human's run of commits closes a bundle when the run ends.
3. **Files inside a bundle may have any names.**

## 7. Style and names

1. **Only the wormstream's own names are binding.** The rest of the house
   style is preference, with justified exceptions, since AnCl3 is meant to
   be cloned and pruned by people who'll laugh at 8.3 and ASCII.
2. **Which time a worm's name gives is a choice**, beyond each author's
   names being monotonic. The record must be true and fair.
3. **A TXT may ask, at its top, to become an MD**: "Please MD all that
   follows", say. That is the say-so; the TXT stays as its human wrote it.
4. **The docs' main links form a DAG**, the laxer requirement, rather than
   a tree.
5. **In conversation, lists are labelled UDld**: uppercase letters, digits,
   lowercase letters, digits. Short replies start at the digits.
6. **When `main` moves under a push**, rebase only if what arrived touches
   none of your files; otherwise stop and look. Never force-push. See also
   [`10222858.md`](10222858.md).
