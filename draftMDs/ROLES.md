# ROLES.md: owners, drones and bees (draft)

Provisional; breaking changes are expected. Refer to
[`TIERS.md`](TIERS.md) for what binds: roles carry duties, and authority
goes with tiers, not with authors.

## 1. Owners

1. **The owners rule jointly.**
2. **Owners may seem to contradict one another.** A session that meets an
   apparent contradiction: (a) doesn't resolve it or blend the two itself;
   (b) quotes both to the owner it is working with, so it stays visible
   until an owner settles it; (c) doesn't let juniority alone decide between
   owners.
3. **The owner in a conversation rules there.** Refer to
   [`TIERS.md`](TIERS.md), sections 3 and 4.
4. **Owners appoint drones.**

## 2. Drones

1. **Drones maintain.** They have a standing assignment to keep the
   operative and draft MDs in order. Only drones and owners edit MDs; only
   humans edit TXTs.
2. It is a duty, not a rank.
3. **Pay particular attention to `CLAUDE.md`**, and revise it in the light
   of experience. It is one of the most critical files in the repo, because
   workers pay particular heed to it. That includes keeping its example path
   (in its Reading section) pointing to whatever worm suits it best.
4. **Watch commit subjects for flags**: failures, warnings and feedback.
   Refer to [`COMMITS.md`](COMMITS.md), section 4.
5. **Drones fill administrative gaps**, even in other authors' folders: a
   bundle's closing worm, for example, or, for a marshalling drone, commits
   made on the bees' behalf. That is editorship, not authorship, and it is
   done only to fill a gap. The rules will adapt to the expedients such
   work turns out to need.
6. **Current drones** are listed in `CLAUDE.md`, 0.3. AnCl9 has its own.

## 3. Bees

1. **Bees create.** They mostly write worms, each in its own author folder.
2. A bee that wants an MD changed says so in a new worm of its own, flagged
   as feedback in the commit's subject line, or asks an owner. No one ever
   edits an existing worm.

## 4. Not natural biology

Our hive breaks with nature. In a honeybee colony the drones are males that
do no work, the workers do all the maintenance, and the queen lays eggs but
doesn't rule; decisions such as where a swarm settles are made collectively
by scouts. Don't reason from the biology.
