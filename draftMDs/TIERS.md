# TIERS.md: which text wins (draft)

Provisional; breaking changes are expected. See also
[`PARADIGM.md`](PARADIGM.md) for the principle these rules serve.

## 1. The tiers

1. **From highest:** (a) root files and `moreMDs/`, the operative docs; (b)
   `draftMDs/`, provisional docs; (c) `UTCworms/`, the record. MDs have
   authority only in (a) and, provisionally, (b), unless a text there
   anoints a worm as a rule. Anointing is a use; citing a worm as evidence
   is only a mention, and confers nothing.
2. **A higher tier beats a lower one**, whatever their dates (lex superior).
3. **Weight goes with the tier, not the author.** A drone's worm is just a
   worm. What a text counts for can be read from its path.
4. **Truth is not authority.** The wormstream is the source of truth, but
   not necessarily of authority.

## 2. The newer text wins (juniority)

1. **Within a tier, the new supersedes the old** (lex posterior).
2. **In the wormstream**, read backwards: a newer worm overrides an older
   one automatically, and the older stays visible underneath.
3. **In the docs**, a newer text replaces the older one by updating it, so
   whatever is there now is the newest.
4. **The tiers never compete on date.** A worm that disputes an operative
   rule changes nothing until someone updates the rule, and that update is
   then the newest text in its tier.
5. **Across authors, order comes from reference.** One text supersedes
   another only by relating to it, and a text can only refer to what is
   already there, so the referring text is, by definition, the newer,
   whatever the clocks say: with clock drift, for example, it may even have
   the earlier name. Unrelated texts need no order. Two that contradict
   without citing each other are settled by whoever notices, in a newer
   text that refers to both.

## 3. The specific beats the general (lex specialis)

1. Only one human takes part in a conversation with a session.
2. So what an owner says in a conversation beats a general rule in the
   repo.

## 4. Rulings made in conversation are recorded

1. A conversation is visible only to its two parties, so owners' rulings
   are recorded in worms.
2. **An override needs confirmation first.** When an owner's instruction
   would override a repo rule, the session points that out and asks that
   same owner for explicit confirmation before acting. Then it mentions it
   in its commit message, or failing that in a worm.

## 5. Correction

1. **As in courts**, a mistake is overruled by a new text, never by editing
   the old one. The overruled text stays on the record, visible but no
   longer binding.
2. **Content moves inwards**, from worms to drafts to operative docs, as it
   becomes dependable. Drafts cite worms rather than copying them.

## 6. What may change where

1. **Write once is the universal principle.** Everything is add-only
   except the constitutive rules, the docs, which are articulated by
   updating them.
2. `UTCworms/`, and any other stream: add only.
3. **If a no-update or no-delete rule bites, create instead**: a new MD in
   your own wormstream folder, saying why. It is the go-to recourse
   whenever a session is otherwise stuck.
4. Root files and `moreMDs/`: add and update; no deleting.
5. `draftMDs/`: add, update and delete. **To retire or invalidate a
   draft, leave it in place and amend it to say so.** Delete only when an
   initiative proves entirely mistaken.

## 7. Commentary

1. **A commit message is its author's word.** One written by a session is
   probably to be taken seriously. One a tool improvised and nobody
   checked is not to be relied on.
2. Commit messages are never corrected, since that would rewrite history. A
   worm corrects them.
3. Refer to [`COMMITS.md`](COMMITS.md) for what commit messages should
   contain, and for raising flags.
